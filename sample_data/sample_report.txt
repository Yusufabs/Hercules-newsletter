HERCULES INTERNAL STUDY: Snack Packaging Preferences, Urban India
Panel: SuperJ verified respondents, n=1,240, metros + tier 2 cities
Fielded: September 2026

OBJECTIVES
Understand how packaging format affects purchase intent and perceived
freshness for a mid-range namkeen snack brand considering a switch from
foil pouches to resealable stand-up pouches.

KEY FINDINGS

1. 71% of respondents said a resealable pack would make them trust the
   snack was "fresher," even when told the product inside was identical.
   This effect held across all age groups but was strongest among
   respondents aged 25-34 (79%).

2. Counterintuitively, price sensitivity did not predict this effect.
   Respondents in the lowest income bracket were just as likely (68%) as
   the highest bracket (73%) to associate resealability with freshness.
   The team expected a stronger effect among higher income respondents.

3. 54% of respondents said they had thrown away a partially finished
   packet of a similar snack in the last month because it "felt stale"
   after opening, even without tasting it first. This suggests a
   meaningful share of category waste is driven by packaging perception
   rather than actual product spoilage.

4. When shown two identical snacks side by side, one in foil and one in
   a resealable pouch, 62% picked the resealable pack as the one they'd
   buy again, and 44% said they'd pay a small premium for it, with a
   median acceptable premium of 8%.

5. Respondents who described themselves as "health conscious" were no
   more or less likely to value resealability than others, suggesting
   this is a general packaging preference rather than a health-driven
   one.

METHODOLOGY NOTE
Respondents were shown side by side product images and asked forced
choice and Likert scale questions. Standard SuperJ panel weighting
applied. Margin of error approximately 2.8 percentage points at 95%
confidence.
